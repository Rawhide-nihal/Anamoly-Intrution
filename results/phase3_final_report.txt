============================================================================================
PHASE 3 — FINAL SYNTHESIS: ISOLATION FOREST AND DBSCAN FOR ANOMALY-BASED INTRUSION DETECTION
CIC-IDS2017 | all values read from frozen Phase 2A/2B/2C artifacts (hash-verified); no model rerun
============================================================================================

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
  * Isolation Forest, trained only on BENIGN flows, reached ROC-AUC 0.8082, precision 0.8387, recall 0.5789 and F1 0.6850 on an unseen test set of 1,054,824 rows.
  * DBSCAN, run transductively on a 50,000-row representative sample, labelled 3.22% of rows as
    density noise; 2.05% of the sample's attack rows were noise (precision 0.1061).
  * Post-hoc, 41.0% of DBSCAN's attack rows sat in 11 attack-dominated clusters and 56.1% in mixed clusters:
    many attacks are dense, not sparse, in this feature space.
  * PCA (19/23/31 components) and removing Destination Port did not materially change DBSCAN's low
    attack recall; removing Destination Port changed Isolation Forest only modestly.
  * The two algorithms operationalise 'anomaly' differently, which explains much of their different behaviour.
  * Isolation Forest was evaluated on an unseen test population after being trained exclusively on BENIGN traffic, whereas DBSCAN was evaluated transductively on a representative mixed sample. Therefore, their raw metric values should be interpreted within their respective experimental designs rather than as a direct apples-to-apples benchmark.

3. EXPERIMENTAL METHODOLOGY
  Isolation Forest (Phase 2A): seeded 70/30 split of BENIGN rows; TRAIN = BENIGN only; TEST = remaining BENIGN +
  all attacks. Median imputer and StandardScaler fitted on TRAIN. Pre-specified configuration; contamination and
  tree-count variations were reported only as sensitivity analyses.
  DBSCAN (Phase 2B): uniform random sample (random_state=42); 100k rows were infeasible at the selected eps
  (~29.3 GB of neighbour storage vs a 10 GB budget), so the documented 50k fallback (first half of
  the same draw) was used. Imputer/scaler fitted on the sample; eps chosen by Kneedle on the log10 k-distance
  curve and min_samples as the candidate closest to ln(n); labels sealed until all clustering finished.
  Phase 2C removed only Destination Port and repeated each primary procedure unchanged.

  TABLE 4 — Confusion matrices (rows: truth; populations differ)

  Isolation Forest flagged 246,560 of 425,878 attack rows (57.9%) while raising false alarms on
  47,421 of 628,946 unseen BENIGN rows (7.54%); 179,318 attack rows were not flagged. Its
  continuous score ranks attacks above benign traffic with ROC-AUC 0.8082. Flag rates were high for several

5. DBSCAN

  procedure — not an 'optimal' eps), min_samples=10; 56 clusters, 1,612 noise rows (3.22%), largest cluster 36,489 rows.
  DBSCAN produced relatively few false positives (1,441 of 41,665 BENIGN rows, FPR 0.0346) but
  8,164 of 8,335 attack rows in its sample were assigned to clusters rather than noise (FNR 0.9795).
  Post-hoc cluster composition (frozen sample; diagnostic only, not used for any selection):
    41.0% of attack rows in 11 highly attack-dominated clusters (>=95% attack)
    56.1% in 4 mixed clusters (5-95% attack), chiefly the largest cluster (36,489 rows, 87.7% BENIGN)

9. COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS
  Anomaly definitions. Isolation Forest: an observation is anomalous when it is relatively easy to isolate through
  randomised tree partitioning. DBSCAN: an observation is anomalous only when it is assigned to density noise (-1).
  Consequences observed here: attacks can receive high Isolation Forest scores even when they belong to dense
  traffic patterns (e.g. DoS Hulk, flagged at 89.3%). This is consistent with the forest being fitted on
  BENIGN data only, where such patterns are rare, whereas DBSCAN, run on a mixed sample, found the same high-volume
  attacks forming dense groups (DoS Hulk 1.0% noise) and so did not label them noise. In short, 'attack' != 'DBSCAN noise', while an attack can still receive a
  high Isolation Forest anomaly score — a central finding of this project.

    Isolation Forest — trained on BENIGN traffic; predicts unseen observations; continuous score with a threshold for
    the binary decision; supports ROC analysis; evaluated on a separate test set.
    DBSCAN — clusters the supplied sample directly; no standard sklearn predict() for unseen observations; noise
    assignment is sample-dependent; no intrinsic continuous score; eps/min_samples materially influence results;
    the primary experiment is transductive. (Adaptations for deployment exist but were not evaluated here.)

11. LIMITATIONS
   1. CIC-IDS2017 is a benchmark/testbed dataset and may not represent production traffic.
   2. The DBSCAN experiment used 50,000 observations because the 100,000-row configuration exceeded the available memory budget.
   3. Isolation Forest and DBSCAN were evaluated under different experimental paradigms.
   4. DBSCAN is transductive in this implementation.
   5. DBSCAN is sensitive to eps and min_samples.
   6. High-dimensional distance structure affects DBSCAN.
   7. PCA changes local-density geometry.
   8. Rare attack categories have unstable detection estimates.
   9. The test attack prevalence (40.4%) is not intended to represent real-world traffic prevalence.
  10. Destination Port was investigated by ablation but not declared leakage.
  11. The experiments evaluate binary anomaly detection rather than attack-type classification.
  12. No real-time production deployment was evaluated.
  13. No external dataset validation was performed.

12. RESEARCH-QUESTION ANSWERS
  RQ1 — Can Isolation Forest detect anomalous traffic when trained only on BENIGN observations?
    Yes, partially. On the unseen test set it reached ROC-AUC 0.8082, precision 0.8387, recall 0.5789, F1 0.6850
    (TP 246,560, FP 47,421, FN 179,318, TN 581,525). Detection was uneven across categories: high for
    volumetric DoS traffic, near zero for PortScan, brute-force (Patator), Bot and web attacks.
  RQ2 — Can DBSCAN identify anomalous traffic by treating low-density observations as noise?
    Only to a very limited extent in this experiment: 3.22% of the sample was noise, with precision 0.1061, recall 0.0205, F1 0.0344.
    Most attack rows were in dense clusters (41.0% attack-dominated, 56.1% mixed). Noise share varied 1.55-11.00%
    across the sensitivity grid, so the result depends on the density parameters; no tested setting changed the
    picture of low attack recall (max recall in the grid 0.129).
  RQ3 — How does Destination Port affect the two algorithms?
    Modestly. Isolation Forest: F1 0.6850 -> 0.6733, ROC-AUC 0.8082 -> 0.8140, score correlation 0.997.
    DBSCAN (eps re-selected 2.552 -> 3.100): noise 3.22% -> 2.30%, recall 0.021 -> 0.012;
    attacks remained predominantly inside clusters. The largest category-level change was DoS GoldenEye, which moved
    in opposite directions for the two algorithms.
  RQ4 — How do the algorithms differ in anomaly-detection behaviour?
    Isolation vs density: Isolation Forest scores how easily a flow separates from BENIGN-trained partitions; DBSCAN
    asks whether a flow lies in a sparse region of the mixed sample. Out-of-sample vs transductive: Isolation Forest
    scored 1,054,824 unseen rows; DBSCAN labelled only the 50,000 rows it clustered. Continuous vs binary: Isolation
    Forest supports ROC analysis; DBSCAN gives noise/cluster only. Computation: Isolation Forest's cost was small and
    linear in scored rows; DBSCAN's neighbour storage limited the sample size. Category behaviour: high-volume DoS
    traffic was largely flagged by Isolation Forest but clustered by DBSCAN.

13. FINAL CONCLUSION
  Isolation Forest produced meaningful anomaly separation under the BENIGN-only training formulation, with ROC-AUC
  0.8082 and F1 0.6850 on its frozen unseen test set. DBSCAN identified a small fraction of observations as
  density noise, but only 2.05% of attack observations in its primary sample were flagged as noise. Post-hoc
  cluster analysis showed that many attacks occurred inside dense clusters rather than as isolated observations. PCA
  did not materially change this DBSCAN behaviour, and Destination Port removal produced only modest changes in the
  Isolation Forest results and did not resolve DBSCAN's low attack recall. The algorithms therefore exhibit different
  anomaly-detection behaviour because they operationalise 'anomaly' differently. These conclusions are specific to
  CIC-IDS2017 and to the frozen experimental designs described above.
